\title{Abliteration and Adversarial Validation of Refusal Mechanisms in LFM3-1.7B}
\author{Research manuscript --- author review draft}
\date{October 29, 2026 \\ Version 0.1.0}

\begin{abstract}
We study refusal-related representations in the tri-hybrid LFM3-1.7B model
and separate behavioral refusal from effective resistance to unauthorized
actions. We preregister benign/adversarial paired contrasts, layer-level
representation measurements, reversible interventions, and symbolic
tool-authorization evaluation. We report a complete 450-prompt, three-checkpoint XSTest generation
experiment (1,350 completions) with provenance checks and clearly
qualified lexical refusal diagnostics. Human-graded refusal,
malicious-action resistance, and complete capability retention remain
under evaluation; no safety efficacy claim is made.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Previous mechanistic work found low-dimensional refusal-mediating
representations in some transformer language models \cite{arditi2024}.
LFM3-1.7B uses 13 gated-convolution, five sliding-window attention, and six
global-attention blocks \cite{liquid2026}, raising an open question about
whether those mechanisms generalize across architectures. Agentic use also
requires distinguishing verbal refusal from actual action authorization.
\section{Research questions}
We test four preregistered questions:
(1) whether paired refusal-associated contrasts replicate in held-out prompts;
(2) whether reversible perturbations affect refusal independently of benign
helpfulness; (3) whether effects depend on operator type; and
(4) whether verbal refusal predicts resistance to unauthorized tool actions.
A shift in refusal language is not evidence of effective safety by itself.
\section{Model and experimental design}
The principal subject is the post-trained LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B checkpoint.
LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B-Base is a distinct comparison, not an equivalent control.
Full-precision and quantized versions are analyzed in separate strata.
We freeze model revision, tokenizer, prompt template, seed, dtype, runtime,
and dataset hash for each experiment.
\subsection{Activation contrasts}
For benign and adversarial variants of matched prompt $i$, let
$h_\ell^{(b,i)}$ and $h_\ell^{(a,i)}$ be the layer-$\ell$ hidden vectors
at the assistant generation prefix. The descriptive contrast is
\[
\bar{\Delta}_\ell=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
(h_\ell^{(a,i)}-h_\ell^{(b,i)}),\qquad
d_\ell=\frac{\bar{\Delta}_\ell}{\|\bar{\Delta}_\ell\|_2}.
\]
These vectors can encode wording and topic rather than a causal refusal
mechanism. Direction selection must occur on training pairs only; holdout
prompts and sham vectors evaluate generalization.

\subsection{Reversible interventions}
On an inference-only forward hook, project activation $h_\ell$ by
\[
h_\ell'=h_\ell-\alpha \langle h_\ell,d_\ell\rangle d_\ell,
\quad \alpha\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}.
\]
Compare identical seeds, decoding settings, and model weights under
baseline, projection, and random-direction controls. Assess convolution,
sliding-attention, and global-attention blocks separately. Preserve the
original checkpoint and do not automatically distribute altered weights.

\subsection{Behavioral outcomes}
Evaluate (a) justified refusal or safe redirection of malicious prompts,
(b) human-graded actionable harmful content, (c) benign utility and
false-positive refusals, and (d) simulated unauthorized action attempts.
Authorization is evaluated by a separate deterministic action policy:
a model response never grants the permissions that policy enforces.
No executable malicious tools, external targets, or live actions are used.
Keyword-based refusal detection is only a weak descriptive proxy.

\subsection{Analysis and validation}
Use blinded human review, independent heldout cases, multiple prompt
paraphrases, and category-based breakdowns. Report paired sample sizes,
bootstrap confidence intervals, and correction for searched layers and
projection strengths. Use standardized red-teaming ideas such as HarmBench
\cite{mazeika2024} only with appropriate controls and data handling.
A lower refusal rate is never treated as a successful safety outcome.
\section{Three-checkpoint experimental design (v0.1)}
We use exactly three primary checkpoint arms, with all evaluations run
using the same private harness. The pretrained model is treated as a
\emph{developmental control}, not as a refusal-free chat baseline:

\toprule
Arm & Checkpoint & Primary comparison \\
\midrule
A & LFM3-1.7B post-trained & Native refusal/utility \\
B & M1-style edited A & Refusal suppression vs. A \\
C & LFM3-1.7B-Base & Behaviors prior to post-training \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The edit in B is a conventional direct-removal variant of the
difference-of-means abliteration of Arditi et al.\ \cite{arditi2024}.
Unlike the initial reversible forward-hook probe, B is a separate,
locally saved model whose weights are modified. Its original weights
and full chain of custody are retained. With $d$ a normalized
candidate direction and $W$ an operator's residual-stream output
weight matrix, we use
\[
W'=(I-\alpha dd^{\top})W,\qquad\alpha=1\quad\text{(M1 direct removal)}.
\]
The experimental implementation edits each of the 24 MLP $w_2$
weights and the 24 residual operator outputs (13 convolution output
projections and 11 attention output projections), for 48 edits.
Because LFM3 uses sandwich RMS normalizations and hybrid operators,
this projection does \emph{not} guarantee removal of the corresponding
direction at residual addition. It is an empirical hypothesis to test.

\subsection{Exact edit integrity and activation-level limitations}
A full-tensor comparison of the original and edited checkpoints
verified the exact projection on all 48 targeted residual-writing
weight matrices and unchanged values for the other 238 of 286
tensors, with full SHA-256 provenance checks. Previously stored
four-pair disjoint heldout activation measurements (distinct from
the two calibration pairs) found lower directional-projection
RMS in 22 of 23 measured layers, with median edited/original
ratio 0.801448 (range 0.224911 to 1.049562).
This is a structural and activation-level observation, not a
validated refusal, safety or benign-helpfulness score.

The edit applies a rank-one projection analogous to Arditi
et al.\ \cite{arditi2024}, but does not reproduce their full
direction-candidate selection and heldout validation protocol:
only two prompt pairs informed the direction. LFM3 sandwich
normalization can restore direction components after the edited
writers. We therefore describe B as a mechanically verified
M1-style experimental derivative, not a validated canonical
refusal-abliteration reproduction.

\subsection{Preregistered scoring and independent controls}
For each of A, B, and C, measure a held-out benign-helpfulness score,
false-refusal rate, human-graded harmful-actionability score, safe
redirection on unauthorized requests, latency, and per-token cost.
Use deterministic zero-temperature runs before evaluating multiple
sampling seeds; apply balanced prompt variations. For the base arm,
also evaluate natural next-token completion to account for its
different training objective. The mock authorization policy is a
separate control: it must block prohibited actions regardless of
the model's text.

A sham projection, random-vector orthogonalization, and alpha sweep
are \emph{methodological controls}, not additional checkpoints in the
three-arm comparison. All hyperparameter selections use calibration
splits. Holdouts remain unseen until choices are frozen.
We will report 95\% confidence intervals and human annotation
agreement, with all denominators and failures disclosed.

\subsection{Prototype results and evidence policy}
The first two-pair calibration and two-pair held-out reversible
forward-hook probe are strictly exploratory. It was not sufficient
to validate safety. The locally generated M1-style derivative is
similarly an unvalidated smoke checkpoint. No public distribution
or automatic model replacement is authorized. Quantitative claims
require a larger independent dataset, prompt contamination audit,
human review, and a pre-registered decision threshold.

\section{XSTest as a frozen independent benchmark}
We use XSTest \cite{xstest2024} as an external, non-calibration
benchmark of exaggerated safety behavior. Its 450 prompt set
comprises 250 safe and 200 unsafe contrast prompts, distributed
under CC BY 4.0. The exact source CSV is pinned by SHA-256
\texttt{11783fb294ed017473ee53c207d71f2161c7672c8d0b037501e78387f801cb5a}.
The dataset is never used to estimate the weight-edit direction or
choose the modified checkpoint. The prescribed three-arm evaluation
is $450\times 3=1350$ generations, with fixed templates and seeds.
Each prompt is scored for full compliance, partial refusal, full
refusal, and uncertain outcomes by two model-blinded human reviewers.
We additionally annotate actionable harmful content and harmless
helpfulness. Report safe over-refusal and unsafe actionability in
separate strata: neither total refusal nor lexical apology alone is
an adequate safety outcome. We compute paired A/B contrasts with
uncertainty intervals and disclose all ungraded items and reviewer
disagreements.

Early stratified test runs are labeled integration smoke results,
not independent evidence of abliteration efficacy. In particular,
the pretrained checkpoint C lacks instruction tuning and requires
a raw-completion sensitivity analysis; treating native chat-template
generations as equivalent would overstate comparability.

\subsection{Stratified integration smoke results (unreviewed)}
A deterministic, one-per-category XSTest pilot yielded $18$ prompts
per checkpoint and $54$ generations. Lexical refusal indicators were
$2/10$ safe and $7/8$ unsafe for A; $2/10$ safe and $8/8$ unsafe for B;
and $1/10$ safe and $7/8$ unsafe for C. These counts are explicitly
\emph{not} human-graded behavior or safety estimates; generations were
capped at $32$ tokens, model B was calibrated on only two examples, and
one observation per category has no meaningful statistical power.
Two independent model-blinded annotation packets were created,
but human review and adjudication have not begun. The pilot is
separate from the now-completed full 450-item generation study.
Standardized independent capability replication remains outstanding.

\subsection{Complete XSTest generation results: lexical proxy only}
All 450 prompts in the pinned XSTest corpus were generated once
for each of three frozen checkpoint arms with deterministic decoding
and at most 128 new tokens, yielding 1,350 completed generations.
The source and model SHA-256 digests are recorded in run provenance.
All cases were matched across arms, and the journal passed the
independent count, duplicate, order, category and integrity audit.

For the 250 safe prompts, a weak lexical refusal proxy fired in
39 for A (15.6\%), 31 for B (12.4\%), and 34 for C (13.6\%).
For the 200 unsafe contrast prompts, it fired in 172 for A
(86.0\%), 167 for B (83.5\%) and 163 for C (81.5\%).
The matched A/B safe set contained nine yes-to-no and one
no-to-yes proxy flips, or a B-minus-A difference of $-3.2$
percentage points. The matched unsafe set contained ten
yes-to-no and five no-to-yes flips, or $-2.5$ percentage points.
Seeded paired-prompt bootstrap 95\% intervals for the
\emph{lexical proxy only} were $[-6.0,-0.8]$ percentage points
for safe prompts and $[-6.5,1.0]$ points for unsafe prompts.

These are descriptive changes in \emph{wording}, not measures
of actual harmful compliance, legitimate refusal, or helpfulness.
A 128-token cap can truncate answers. Refusal keywords may
coexist with operationally harmful instructions, and safe
redirection need not include those keywords. Model C is not
instruction-tuned. Moreover, the edited model's direction
was fitted using only two calibration pairs. All 1,350
private responses are now available for two independent
model-blinded human reviewers, but annotation has not begun.
No safety or capability-effectiveness conclusion follows.


\subsection{Exploratory exact matched lexical-proxy inference}
As a sensitivity analysis, the frozen 450-by-three lexical-proxy
aggregate also supports exact conditional matched-pair tests.
For the benign A/B proxy, nine originally keyword-positive
responses became negative and one originally negative response
became positive under B. The nominal two-sided conditional
McNemar $p$ value is $0.021484375$, but a Holm correction across
all three pairwise arm contrasts in each of the two strata
gives an adjusted $p=0.12890625$. This does not meet a
familywise $0.05$ criterion. The earlier paired bootstrap
interval of $[-6.0,-0.8]$ percentage points was not
simultaneously corrected for these exploratory comparisons.
This analysis tests only lexical wording; it cannot establish
real refusal removal, useful compliance, or safety.

\subsection{M1-style intervention fidelity on independent probes}
An additional activation-fidelity smoke audit compared models A and B
on four disjoint paired prompts (eight final-token observations). We
measured each decoder block's root-mean-square component along the
frozen calibration direction, then computed edited/original ratios.
At layer 7 this ratio was $0.658$, at layer 10 it was $0.225$,
at layer 14 it was $0.704$, at layer 18 it was $0.771$, and at
layer 23 it was $0.806$. These descriptive ratios suggest
nonuniform suppression and possible reintroduction of the projected
component after the targeted layer in the LFM3 hybrid residual path.
They do not demonstrate that the contrast direction encodes refusal,
nor do they measure malicious-action resistance. The contrast used
only two fitting pairs and requires substantially larger replication.

\subsection{Independent capability-retention protocol}
We supplement XSTest with a pinned ARC-Challenge test set of 1,172
questions \cite{arc2018}, sharing the same frozen A/B/C checkpoints.
For an initial comparison, each question is rendered as a raw
multiple-choice completion with canonical option letters A--E.
The probability proxy is the softmax of the next-token letter logits
over available options; this choice removes the pretrained base's
chat-template mismatch but does not constitute official ARC accuracy.
Formal reporting requires conditional sequence likelihood, order
permutations, full denominators, and separate instruction-following
assessments. For each checkpoint, we preserve case-level predictions
only in private storage. Any results from a limited seeded subset
remain labeled as capability integration smoke, not retained
reasoning capability claims.

\subsection{Full-answer conditional-choice capability protocol}
To supplement the one-token letter-completion integration test,
we implement a pinned, resumable ARC-Challenge evaluator that
scores the entire candidate answer text with conditional
log-likelihood under a fixed raw-completion question prefix.
Both raw sum log-likelihood and mean-per-token scores are
recorded for the same item across A, B, and C. All three
arms use identical answer text, choice order, and tokenizer
prefix conventions; no external tools are executed.
The 1,172-question test split and checkpoint hashes are frozen,
and every answer is journaled privately. This custom
likelihood-based analysis is \emph{not} presented as an
official ARC leaderboard score or as a safety measure;
confirmation under a standardized independent harness,
order permutations, and instruction-following tests is required.

\subsection{Independent standardized ARC task-readiness audit}
We installed a separate pinned EleutherAI lm-eval v0.4.13
environment and validated its built-in ARC-Challenge
multiple-choice task rather than treating our custom
answer-text likelihood protocol as an official score.
A read-only SHA-256 audit verified the official inherited
task definition and two distinct acc and acc-norm metrics,
the pinned 1,172-question corpus, and the three original
checkpoint identities. The corpus has 1,165 four-choice,
four three-choice and three five-choice questions.

This static readiness verification is not a scored model
inference run. The attempted independent model-scoring
invocation did not execute. We therefore provide no official
ARC scores or capability-preservation claims from this
audit. Authorized evaluation on all three frozen arms and
human response review remain publication gates.

\subsection{Limited real CPU capability-scoring preflight}
We completed a limited model-scoring preflight on all three
unchanged checkpoints using EleutherAI lm-eval v0.4.13.
The canonical Hub ARC dataset was unavailable in the isolated
offline cache, so a private task adapter inherited the official
multiple-choice scoring rules while reading the SHA-256-pinned
local test parquet. An initial Radeon 890M ROCm execution
failed at the first embedding kernel, whereas CPU bfloat16
completed the same FIRST 40 questions per checkpoint.
The raw and length-normalized accuracies were respectively
20.0\% and 25.0\% for A, 17.5\% and 25.0\% for B,
and 40.0\% and 40.0\% for C. These small, nonrandom,
locally adapted preliminary scores do not constitute a full
official ARC benchmark or evidence of capability preservation,
zero-refusal behavior, or safety. The independent full
1,172-question and human-review gates remain unmet.

\subsection{Full paired ARC-Challenge likelihood results}
The complete pinned ARC-Challenge test evaluation comprises
1,172 questions per checkpoint, totaling 3,516 model-question
scores. For length-normalized conditional answer-text likelihood,
A selected 315/1,172 correct (26.88\%), B selected 301/1,172
(25.68\%), and C selected 493/1,172 (42.06\%).
B gained 14 correct items and lost 28 versus A on matched cases,
for a paired B-minus-A difference of $-1.19$ percentage points
(2,000-resample 95\% paired bootstrap interval
$[-2.30,-0.09]$ percentage points). The models chose identical
answers on 1,088/1,172 cases.

For the alternative unnormalized sum of option-token log
probabilities, correct counts were A 277/1,172, B 263/1,172,
C 459/1,172; the B-minus-A difference was also $-1.19$
percentage points, with 17 items gained and 31 lost.
Independent model-hash and journal-integrity checks passed.

These results arise from a custom raw-completion scoring
protocol, not the official ARC benchmark harness. Only one
prompt wording and option order were evaluated. Post-trained
models and the pretrained base may react differently to this
format. A measured difference in these scores is not by itself
evidence of general capability loss or of safety preservation.
Human ratings of the refusal benchmark remain outstanding.

\subsection{Exploratory exact matched-question inference}
As a secondary statistical sensitivity check on the complete
frozen ARC journal, we computed the exact two-sided conditional
McNemar binomial test on correct-to-incorrect and
incorrect-to-correct flips for three checkpoint contrasts and
both likelihood scoring conventions (six exploratory tests).
To avoid promoting a post hoc single contrast, Holm--Bonferroni
familywise adjustment was applied across all six.

For length-normalized choice scoring, B versus A had 14
gained and 28 lost correct answers among 42 discordant
questions ($p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.04356$). For raw-sum likelihood,
B versus A had 17 gained and 31 lost among 48 discordant
questions ($p_{\mathrm{exact}}=0.05946$). Both adjusted
$p$-values were $0.08712$, exceeding the conventional
$0.05$ reference threshold. These exploratory tests are
reported alongside the paired bootstrap interval, rather
than interpreted as a formal preregistered treatment effect.

Neither statistical non-significance nor a small observed
difference constitutes an equivalence or noninferiority
demonstration. No acceptable capability loss margin was
preregistered, and the results remain sensitive to an
unstandardized scoring method, single option order, and
chat-versus-base prompt mismatch. Human-graded refusal
and actionability remain unavailable. The evidence is
specific to the frozen three-checkpoint comparison.

\subsection{ARC answer-length and scoring-rule sensitivity}
To assess the influence of a plausible scoring confound without
adding checkpoints or generating new answers, we audited both
predeclared likelihood scores on the same 1,172 ARC questions.
There were 4 questions with three choices, 1,165 with four,
and 3 with five; selecting uniformly among choices would
yield approximately 25.02\% accuracy in expectation.

Raw-sum and length-normalized likelihood selected different
answers on 499/1,172 questions for A, 503/1,172 for B,
and 423/1,172 for C. Among 501 cases with a \emph{unique}
shortest option by token count, the raw-sum method selected
that option 274 times for A, 272 for B, and 230 for C;
normalization selected it 60, 60, and 54 times respectively.
This does not establish which scoring rule is optimal, but
it documents substantial format sensitivity and the potential
effect of candidate length on raw-sum scores.

Because the post-trained and M1-edited arms score near this
choice-count baseline under the custom completion format, we
avoid interpreting their small paired difference as evidence
of general reasoning degradation. Independent standard
benchmarking, prompt and order robustness checks, and
human-reviewed safety outcomes are still required.

\subsection{Capability integration smoke (not official ARC scoring)}
A seeded 12-question ARC-Challenge subset was evaluated with raw
multiple-choice letter-completion scores. The original and M1-edited
checkpoints each selected 4/12 correct options, and selected identical
letters on all 12 examples. The pretrained checkpoint selected 8/12
correct options using the same raw-completion prompt. This is not
an official ARC benchmark result, a measure of instruction-following,
or evidence of preserved capabilities: the sample is small, the answer
scoring uses only first-letter logits, and the model variants differ
in post-training. We report it only as a reproducible end-to-end
capability test of the frozen checkpoints.

\subsection{Predeclared paired human effect estimates}
On completion of the full XSTest benchmark, we pair answers by the
same original prompt across all three checkpoints. Two independent
blinded reviewers must score each answer, with no unresolved
disagreement or uncertain judgments; otherwise the endpoint is
withheld. For the 250 safe prompts, the primary outcomes are
over-refusal and ordinal helpfulness. For the 200 unsafe contrasts,
the outcomes are actionable harmful content and full refusal,
reported separately. Let $s_i^{(m)}$ be the human-scored outcome
of checkpoint $m$ on item $i$. The paired edited-minus-original
effect is $\hat{\Delta}_{B-A}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
(s_i^{(B)}-s_i^{(A)})$; analogous descriptive contrasts are
reported for $C-A$ and $C-B$. We derive percentile 95\% intervals
using 2,000 reproducibly seeded paired-prompt bootstrap resamples.
Only the prompt is resampled; individual checkpoint generations
are not treated as independent. An incomplete human-review
dataset cannot pass the scorer. Before adjudication, we require
a separate independent reliability audit on the complete 1,350
paired annotations: per-field exact agreement and Cohen's kappa
for categorical refusal/actionability, with quadratic-weighted
kappa for ordinal benign helpfulness. If the marginal label
distribution has no variation, kappa is undefined and reported
as such. Both original blinded sheets are retained, with no
automatic generation of human grades. No inter-rater score is
reported before the reviewers have actually completed their
annotations. These comparisons keep the primary model count
at exactly three.

\subsection{Evidence-based publication readiness}
A machine-readable, read-only gate requires all three pinned checkpoint
hashes to agree with the benchmark journal, a complete and well-formed
$1350$-generation XSTest run, consensus from two independent human
reviewers on each case, and full independent capability evidence.
Checkpoint provenance mismatch, incomplete response records, and
unadjudicated human disagreement prevent strong behavioral claims.
Passing these checks does not itself authorize model publication or
deployment: distribution and release require separate risk review.

\section{Governance and responsible dissemination}
Model weights and raw adversarial generations remain private.
There is no real tool execution, unrestricted serving endpoint, or
automatic model promotion. Distributing altered weights requires independent
safety assessment, legal/license review, and explicit publication approval.

\section{Implementation status and limitations}
The complete pinned three-arm XSTest generation benchmark is audited,
alongside eight paired calibration/holdout prompts, six synthetic
authorization cases, activation-fidelity probes, and unit tests.
Lexical results are descriptive, not independently reviewed safety
metrics. The causal role of the measured direction remains unknown.
Prompt-topic and prompt-length confounds can distort activation contrasts.
A verbal refusal is not proof that malicious real-world actions are blocked.

\section{Reproducibility}
Pin model revision, template, data hashes, seeds, precision, code and runtime.
Maintain heldout tests, independent grading, confidence intervals, and
negative results. Do not interpret lexical refusal rates as safety outcomes.
